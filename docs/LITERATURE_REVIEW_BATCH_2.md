# Literature review batch 2 — expanded evidence map

**Review status:** targeted literature expansion, not a completed systematic review. Search date: 2026-10-25. Search sources included PubMed-indexed records, publisher full text, and Zenodo records. Search strings and screening decisions still need to be logged before any PRISMA-style claims.

## Why this batch matters

The literature is already moving beyond basic PD-versus-control classification. Reviews and recent primary studies repeatedly flag external validation, participant-level leakage, dataset shift, demographic confounding, calibration, and clinically meaningful outcomes as unresolved concerns. At least one 2026 reproducibility report directly studies participant leakage and cross-dataset transfer in two standard voice-feature datasets, so our potential contribution cannot simply be “test cross-dataset generalization.”

## Synthesis: what we can and cannot claim

- **Cannot claim as novel without a more specific gap:** voice-based PD classification; cross-language evaluation in general; demographic bias analysis in general; participant-level splitting; or cross-dataset testing in isolation.
- **Potentially defensible direction (still provisional):** a prespecified, independently reproducible evaluation of *calibration and clinical operating characteristics under dataset/domain shift*, with explicit participant-level separation and carefully bounded demographic analyses, using datasets whose labels and metadata actually support the comparisons.
- **Critical interpretive rule:** a performance drop on a different language dataset cannot be attributed to language alone if site, device, task, recruitment, age, sex, severity, or preprocessing also change. Describe it as dataset/domain shift unless those factors can be separated.
- **Potential blocker:** several public datasets expose only engineered acoustic features, have very small participant counts, or provide incomplete metadata. That may prevent audio foundation-model work, calibration subgroup analyses, or causal claims about language.
- **Clinical framing:** this project concerns research-stage screening/assessment support, not autonomous diagnosis. A usable screening tool would need prospective and external clinical validation beyond retrospective public datasets.
